Why minimum payments are a trap

Most cards set the minimum at interest plus about 1% of the balance, or a flat 2 to 3% of the balance. Because the payment shrinks as the balance shrinks, the debt lingers: a $6,800 balance at 25% APR paid at the minimum can take well over a decade and cost more in interest than the original purchases.

A fixed payment, keeping the dollar amount constant even as the balance falls, is the simplest escape, and every extra dollar goes straight to principal.

Payoff strategies

  • Avalanche: pay extra on the highest-APR card first, the mathematically cheapest route.
  • Snowball: clear the smallest balance first for momentum; costs slightly more interest.
  • Balance transfers: 0% intro APR offers help only if the balance is gone before the promo ends, and mind the 3 to 5% transfer fee.
  • Stop new charges on any card you are paying down, or the finish line keeps moving.

Credit card payoff FAQs

How long does it take to pay off a credit card?
It depends on the balance, the APR, and how much you pay each month. Paying only the minimum (often 2 to 3% of the balance) can stretch repayment over many years because most of the payment goes to interest. A fixed monthly payment well above the minimum clears the balance much faster.
Why are minimum payments a trap?
Most cards set the minimum at interest plus about 1% of the balance, or a flat 2 to 3% of the balance. Because the payment shrinks as the balance shrinks, the debt lingers: a $6,800 balance at 25% APR paid at the minimum can take well over a decade and cost more in interest than the original purchases.
What is the debt avalanche vs. snowball method?
The avalanche method puts extra payments toward the highest-APR balance first, which is the mathematically cheapest route. The snowball method clears the smallest balance first for psychological momentum, at a slightly higher interest cost.
Do balance transfer cards help pay off debt?
A 0% introductory APR balance transfer can help, but only if the balance is fully paid before the promo period ends. Watch the 3 to 5% transfer fee, and avoid adding new charges to the old card.
